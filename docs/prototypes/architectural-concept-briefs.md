# Architectural concept briefs — review draft

Throwaway planning prototype. **Proposed, not approved.** This answers [Define the three architectural concept briefs](https://github.com/danielluis07/habitta/issues/11). All three concepts belong in the collection; they are not competing replacements for one another.

Open [the comparison board](architectural-concept-comparison.svg) in a browser, or view [the PNG](architectural-concept-comparison.png). No install or server is needed. The drawings communicate massing, placement, and room relationships; they are not measured plans or final concept visualizations. This is an architectural comparison, not a new website layout experiment.

## Shared district proposal

North is uphill; south opens toward a cloud-filled valley. Crest occupies the northwest ridge, Contour the eastern midslope, and Grove the southwest lower bench. Separate plots connect to a winding public lane. A pedestrian path links the plots through grasses and small groups of trees. Terrain remains visible beneath every building. The cloud layer stays below the occupied terraces in the reference views.

For the eventual source scene, use east/north/up coordinates in metres, with a local, fictional datum: Crest plot centre (0, 100, 32), Contour (90, 30, 16), Grove (-45, -60, 0). These are composition anchors, not survey data. Use an overall district extent of roughly 260 by 300 m. Crest and Contour have road access from the uphill/north edge; Grove from its west edge. The buildings have separate foundations and plot boundaries. Retaining walls explain the change in grade at Contour and Grove; no floating bases or shared megastructure.

Use one warm, late-morning reference sun from the southeast. Keep a neutral warm grade, pale highland grasses, scattered small broadleaf trees, and a distant southern ridge across the valley. The geographic setting is invented and makes no climate or local-code claim. Camera variation must preserve this orientation and the relative positions of the other buildings. A southern view from any residence looks toward valley and cloud; looking north from Grove can reveal Crest, while Contour is northeast of Grove.

## Crest

### Building

A compact tower on a stone ridge plinth. Twelve consistent occupied levels at approximately 3.5 m floor-to-floor, with a flat roof and no additional crown volume. Three broad bays organize the south elevation. Pale mineral concrete piers run vertically; horizontal floor edges sit back. Each residential floor has a recessed loggia at the southwest bay. Dark bronze frames and deep reveals keep the building readable at district scale. The ground level contains the north arrival lobby and support spaces, with the stair/lift core at the north centre. Piers and floor plates align down to the plinth.

### Featured residence

Level 10 is the third level down from the roof. Enter the south-half home from the north-centre common core into a short hall. The kitchen and dining area occupy the northwest of the home and connect openly to the southwest living room. The living room opens south into its recessed loggia. The main bedroom occupies the southeast corner, with a south window; the second bedroom sits northeast with an east window. Bathrooms, laundry, and storage sit against the internal north wall, reached from the hall. Both bedrooms have independent hall access, with no route through another bedroom. The loggia is accessed only from the living room; the bedroom facade stays flush.

The loggia has a solid pale parapet with a slim bronze top rail and the ceiling of the level above. It is recessed into the main volume, never a projecting glass balcony. The living opening is a three-panel bronze sliding assembly; the main bedroom has one broad fixed pane plus a narrower operable light. Window and door divisions are fixed in the source scene and repeated in every image.

### Materials and editorial idea

Exterior: warm pale mineral concrete; grey-beige local-looking stone at the plinth; dark bronze metalwork. Interior: pale oak floor and cabinetry, chalk mineral plaster, and warm grey limestone at the kitchen worktop and loggia paving. Material-study subject: the actual living-to-loggia threshold, where oak, limestone, bronze, and the concrete reveal meet. Stone remains matte and oak light; neither becomes white marble or dark walnut in later images.

Story direction: height experienced through a sheltered, ordinary domestic room. The design tension is between the long horizon and the close depth of a loggia. Working residence name: **Horizon residence**.

## Contour

### Building

Four long residential bars terrace down the eastern midslope. Each is approximately 36 m east-west by 12 m north-south; the bar above shifts 6 m north and rises approximately 3.5 m. Their combined footprint extends approximately 30 m down the slope. The unbuilt southern half of each lower roof forms the terrace for the level above. Level 1 sits on a stone retaining base and has ground terraces. Level 4 has a flat planted roof, not a fifth occupied level. A stair/lift spine on the north side connects all levels to arrival from the uphill lane. Vertical supports align in the 6 m overlap between bars; stone side walls meet the terrain.

Warm, board-textured concrete floor edges establish the stepped silhouette. Recessed glazing and timber screens divide each long front into distinct homes. Stone retaining walls belong to the ground connection, not to arbitrary decorative cladding. Terrace planting uses low, contained beds; no mature trees perched on thin roof edges.

### Featured residence

The western home on level 2 opens to its own deep south terrace on the roof of level 1. A north hall receives the entry from the common spine. Kitchen/dining and living run along the south front; the living room occupies the southwest corner and opens through a four-panel sliding assembly onto the terrace. The main bedroom sits southeast with its own south-facing opening onto that same terrace. Two smaller bedrooms sit along the west side with west windows, independently reached from the internal hall. Bathrooms, laundry, and storage collect on the north side. The terrace has a fixed dining zone by the living room, a low planted edge toward the valley, and a screened quiet zone by the main bedroom. A continuous guard sits behind the planters; vegetation is not the safety barrier.

The west end is important: this home has side light unavailable to a middle residence. The outer terrace edge is open to sky, while a narrow strip near the living opening is sheltered by the floor edge above. The view toward the northwest can include Crest; the primary south view opens to the valley. There is no pool and no duplex staircase in this home.

### Materials and editorial idea

Exterior: warm grey board-textured concrete, rough grey-beige retaining stone, bronze frames, and oiled timber privacy screens. Interior: warm sand-toned terrazzo in living/circulation, light oak in bedrooms, natural oak kitchen fronts, and off-white plaster. Terrace paving is textured sand-coloured stone. Material-study subject: the living threshold and adjacent timber screen, showing terrazzo-to-stone transition and concrete overhead.

Story direction: daily life stretches sideways along a terrace that follows the hill. Its outdoor room is deeper and more inhabited than Crest's loggia. Working residence name: **Terrace residence**.

## Grove

### Building

A two-storey courtyard block on the lower, flatter southwestern bench. A roughly 36 × 30 m perimeter encloses a 16 × 12 m open-to-sky planted court. A passage through the centre of the south wing enters the court without breaking the upper-storey ring. Residents reach the upper homes from two stair cores at the northwest and southeast corners and an enclosed common circulation band. The featured ground-floor residence has its own entrance from the court-side path. A light brick plinth and lime-rendered upper walls give the low volume weight; repeated deep punched openings and timber shutters distinguish it from the glazed Contour frontage. Low parapets hide flat roofs; there are no pitched roofs, third storey, or roof garden.

The central court contains one modest broadleaf tree and a shared planted garden. A perimeter path stays outside the private patio boundaries. The public lane ends at the west entrance forecourt; cars never occupy the planted court. A low retaining wall supports the south edge of the plot.

### Featured residence

The ground-floor northeast corner home wraps two sides of its private patio on the court's northeast edge. Entry from the shared court-side path leads to a small vestibule and central hall. The living/dining room is on the west, opening west onto the private patio; the kitchen sits immediately north of dining. Both bedrooms face the outer east garden, reached separately from the central hall. Bathrooms, laundry, and storage occupy the internal north/service edge. The main bedroom is southeast, with one deep east-facing window and a timber shutter. The smaller bedroom is northeast with a separate east-facing window. No bedroom circulation crosses the living room or patio.

The patio belongs only to this residence. A low brick wall, timber gate, and planting separate it from the common path and shared court beyond. It is open to sky, not an enclosed conservatory. Living glazing is a two-panel timber-framed sliding opening into a deep rendered reveal. Bedrooms look outward to the private plot-edge planting; living looks inward toward the shared garden. The home's two outlooks must not be swapped in imagery.

### Materials and editorial idea

Exterior: warm pale lime render, sandy buff brick plinths and patio walls, timber shutters and frames. Interior: light clay tile in living/kitchen, pale oak in bedrooms, off-white mineral plaster, and oak joinery. Patio paving uses the same clay family with a slip-resistant exterior finish. Material-study subject: clay threshold, timber frame, render reveal, and the adjacent brick patio wall. No exposed concrete frame or bronze tower rail is introduced here.

Story direction: a small domestic sequence from shared garden to private patio to living room, with bedrooms turning toward a quieter outer garden. Working residence name: **Garden residence**.

## Diagram contract

The board's plans are relationship diagrams. North is up. A rust outline identifies the featured home in the massing views. In the plans, labelled rectangles identify rooms; gaps denote connections; blue strokes denote major exterior openings. A labelled arrow identifies entry. Internal halls independently serve all bedrooms and wet/service rooms. Common circulation is outside the home boundary. Final model drawings must preserve these adjacencies and opening directions, with real doors, wall thicknesses and circulation widths resolved during modeling.

The board's elevations share an approximate vertical scale so the 42 m / 14 m / 7 m hierarchy is apparent. Floor-plan panels are independently scaled for readability. The district inset is schematic; the coordinate anchors above govern eventual placement. The drawings omit detailed structural systems and fixtures. They do not certify structure, accessibility, egress, or buildability.

## Review decision

Review the three concepts as one collection. The proposed choice is to retain all three names, silhouettes, palettes, home arrangements, and role briefs above. The strongest contrasts are **high and sheltered**, **stepped and outward**, and **low and inward**.

Please identify any concept that should change, and whether the change concerns its name, building form/scale, residence arrangement, or materials. Acceptance approves a planning brief; final models, images, construction detailing and production copy follow separately. After feedback, the accepted answer belongs in the issue's resolution comment; the map receives only a short linked gist.
